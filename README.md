# Power BI DAX Business Case Study

A practical **Power BI DAX business case study** designed to strengthen DAX fundamentals, filter context, customer analysis, revenue analysis, and advanced analytical calculations.

## 📊 Project Overview

This project contains **17 business-oriented DAX problems** based on an e-commerce sales dataset.

The objective is to solve realistic business questions using Power BI and DAX rather than focusing only on syntax.

The case study progresses from:

**Basic DAX → Iterator Functions → CALCULATE & Filter Context → Customer Analysis → Advanced DAX**

---

## 🗂️ Dataset

The project uses two related tables:

### `Orders`

Contains transaction-level sales information:

* `order_id`
* `order_date`
* `customer_id`
* `category`
* `quantity`
* `unit_price`
* `order_status`

### `Customers`

Contains customer information:

* `customer_id`
* `customer_name`
* `customer_segment`

Relationship:

```text
Customers
    1
    │
    │
    *
Orders
```

`Customers[customer_id]` is the unique customer key, while `Orders[customer_id]` can repeat because a customer can place multiple orders.

# 🎯 Business Questions

## Part 1 — Basic DAX

### Q1. Total Units Sold

Calculate the total quantity of items sold across all orders.

### Q2. Total Number of Orders

Calculate the total number of order transactions.

### Q3. Unique Purchasing Customers

Calculate the number of unique customers who have placed at least one order.

### Q4. Average Quantity per Order

Calculate the average number of items sold per order while safely handling division by zero.

---

## Part 2 — Iterator Functions

### Q5. Total Gross Revenue

Calculate revenue by multiplying quantity by unit price for each order and summing the results.

### Q6. Average Order Value

Calculate the average revenue generated per order.

### Q7. Largest Single Order Line

Find the highest revenue generated by a single order line.

### Q8. Discounted Revenue Simulation

Calculate revenue after applying a 10% discount to order lines where quantity is 3 or more.

---

## Part 3 — CALCULATE & Filter Context

### Q9. Validated Revenue

Calculate revenue only for orders with `Completed` status.

### Q10. High-Tier Customer Revenue

Calculate revenue generated by customers belonging to the `Gold` or `Platinum` segments.

### Q11. Premium Electronics Volume

Calculate units sold for Electronics orders where quantity is greater than 2.

---

## Part 4 — Customer & Date Analysis

### Q12. Customer Segment Stamp

Create a calculated column that retrieves the customer's segment from the Customers table.

### Q13. Churned / Inactive Customers

Count customers who have not placed an order during the previous 180 days, using the maximum order date as the reference date.

---

## Part 5 — Advanced Fresher DAX

### Q14. Customer Revenue Ranking

Rank customers based on their total gross revenue.

### Q15. Customer Percentage Contribution

Calculate each customer's percentage contribution to total company revenue.

### Q16. Top 3 Customers Revenue

Calculate the combined revenue generated by the three highest-revenue customers.

### Q17. Segment Average Benchmark

Calculate the average revenue per customer within the current customer's customer segment while ignoring the individual customer filter.

# 🔄 Learning Approach

The project follows a progression from simple calculations to more advanced DAX logic:

```text
Basic Aggregations
        ↓
Iterator Functions
        ↓
CALCULATE
        ↓
Filter Context
        ↓
Customer Analysis
        ↓
Ranking
        ↓
Contribution Analysis
        ↓
Top N Analysis
        ↓
Segment Benchmarking
```

The focus is not only on **writing DAX**, but also on understanding **why the calculation works and how filter context changes the result**.
